Confirmed, reported or rumour? How to check GTA VI news

Updated 15 Nov 2026 · Independent fan guide, not affiliated with Rockstar Games

Launch week is full of claims. Some are official, some come from credible reporters, and some are made up for clicks. VICEVERSE labels every news item so you can tell the difference at a glance.

Our three labels

  • Confirmed: stated by Rockstar or the platform holder (for example Xbox Wire).
  • Reported: from a credible outlet, but not confirmed by Rockstar. It may change.
  • Rumour: unverified. Treat it as speculation.

A five-step checklist

  1. Find the primary source. Is there a Rockstar post, store listing or platform announcement? If not, the claim is at best reported.
  2. Check the date. Old stories get recycled. A delay or price from last year may no longer apply.
  3. Count independent sources. Ten sites quoting one anonymous post are still one source.
  4. Watch the wording. Phrases such as "reportedly", "sources say" or "could" signal that something is not confirmed.
  5. Be wary of leaks. Leaked footage and documents are often fake or outdated. We do not publish leaks.

A real example

This month, one outlet reported the game had gone gold. That is useful, but we labelled it reported because Rockstar had not said so. By contrast, the 19 November release date is confirmed because Rockstar announced it. The question of a PC version stays a rumour because nothing has been announced.
